Why VinX

Specialised, so simple and fast.

No smart contracts, no stablecoins, no token sprawl. VinX does one thing, and does it well: payments.

Final, right away

Byzantine fault-tolerant (BFT) consensus: once a block is signed by more than 2/3 of the validators, it is irreversible. No waiting for confirmations.

Lightweight

Nodes keep only 30 days of history and a compact, verifiable state. A validator runs on a PC, a mini-server or a Raspberry Pi.

Built for commerce

Invoice references signed with the payment, readable name@domain addresses, and receipts verifiable offline, even after pruning.

Open to everyone

Anyone can become a validator with a 1,000 VINX bond. No permission to ask for, and a network that discovers itself.

Become a validator

A switch. Not a data centre.

In the app, turn on “Validate”: your computer joins the network, posts the bond and starts signing blocks.

  • Rewarded for your commitment. Rewards follow your bond and your uptime: the more you stake and the more you are there, the more you earn.
  • Your funds stay safe. The validator has its own keys; your wallet stays encrypted and is never handed to the node.
  • Works behind a home router. Automatic port opening (UPnP), relays and NAT hole punching: no network setup needed.
  • Forgiving. An offline validator is paused without losing money and reinstates itself when it comes back. Only double voting is penalised.

Economics

A fixed supply, earned by work.

1 billion VINX at most, never more. No presale on mainnet: VINX are created over time and paid to those who run the network.

  • Decreasing issuance. Half the supply is issued over 10 years, then half of the rest, on a smooth curve with no steps.
  • Fees redistributed. Nothing is burned: fees go to the validators.
  • 1,000 VINX bond. The deposit to validate, returned after a 21-day delay.

Built to last

A network that absorbs the unexpected.

Validators come and go; the network must stay up and evolve without ever starting over.

A stable validator set

Capped entries per epoch, a 3-hour warm-up, and offline validators leave the vote at once: failures spread over time do not stop the chain.

Upgrades without restarts

A new version only activates once more than 2/3 of the validators run it. Old blocks stay valid, forever.

No central server

Nodes discover each other and remember their peers: the network survives the loss of any entry point.

Developers

Integrate payments in a few lines.

A simple HTTP API, a TypeScript SDK and receipts verifiable offline. The code is open source, written in Rust.

  • REST API: balances, transactions, blocks, state proofs, event stream.
  • Receipts: proof that a payment is in a block, verifiable without a node.
  • References: 32 bytes signed with the payment, to match an invoice.
// Check that an invoice is paid

const vinx = new VinxClient("https://seed.vinx…");
const tx = await vinx.tx(hash);

if (tx.memo === "INV-2026-0412"
    && tx.to === MY_ADDRESS) {
  const receipt = await vinx.paymentReceipt(hash);
  verifyPaymentReceipt(receipt, hash); // offline proof
}

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

What makes VinX different from other blockchains?

VinX only does payments. Without smart contracts or extra tokens, the protocol stays small, nodes stay light and every payment is final in the next block. It is a rail, not a platform.

What does “final” mean?

A block is only added once signed by more than 2/3 of the voting power. Once added, it cannot be undone: no reorganisation, no confirmations to wait for.

Do I need a powerful computer to validate?

No. Nodes keep only 30 days of blocks and a compact state. A desktop PC, a mini-server or a Raspberry Pi is enough; what matters is that it stays on and connected.

What happens if my validator goes offline?

It is paused: it leaves the vote, loses no money, and earns no rewards in the meantime. When it comes back, it reinstates itself and rejoins the network.

Do testnet VINX have any value?

No. The testnet is for putting the network through its paces; its VINX are handed out free by the faucet and have no value.

Is the code open source?

Yes, it is public on GitHub, written in Rust. Design decisions are documented in ADRs (Architecture Decision Records).
